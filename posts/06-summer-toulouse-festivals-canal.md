# Summer in Toulouse: Beaches by the River, Cinema Under the Stars, and Festivals

[IMAGE]

Most people think Toulouse is a winter business city. They're wrong in the best possible way. When the sun is out, Toulouse turns into a soft, pink-brick, water-friendly city where the river banks *become beaches*, the squares become open-air cinemas, and the calendar is a running festival.

I spent a full summer week there and I have one consistent thought: *Toulouse in summer is a different city from the one you read about.*

## Rabasan Plage — a "beach" by the river

[IMAGE]

**Rabasein Plage** — the classic summer river beach in the middle of the city, by the river. It's not the Mediterranean, of course — but it's a beach in a city, with the sun, the water, the flat sand-gazebo, the people, and the cold drink. Sunbeds appear in the summer. It's oddly, wonderfully enough.

[IMAGE]

**The trick:** for real, arriving after 4pm; the heat and the beach peak in late afternoon. Bring something to read.

## Outdoor cinema and the festival summer

[IMAGE]

Toulouse does open-air cinema properly. Throughout summer, park nights turn into screens — the **Cinescope** and various parks screen free films. Bring a picnic blanket and a bottle of something cold, and you have a city of well-lit thousands.

Then the festivals:

- **Rio Loco** — the world-music festival on the riverbank. International, vibrant, hands-on, big musical guests from across the world. It's the centrepiece.
- **July 14th (Bastille Day), the 14-Juillet** — the big summer festival: the firemen's ball, the dancing, the crowds in the old streets, and from the bridge you see the fireworks over the river. It's the most French of all days to be in France.
- **Plages and the smaller summer events** — the summer weekends are full of them.

## The Canal du Midi

[IMAGE]

The **Canal du Midi** — the great 17th-century canal that links the Atlantic to the Mediterranean — runs through and around Toulouse. In summer it's lined with trees, always the coolest walk in the city, with the locks, the towpath, the cyclists, and the barge.

**Try:** the canal-side walk or bike from the centre to the newer reaches; or a **barge/boat** holiday feels distant, but you can feel a little of it by taking the towpath at dusk. It's the most tranquil part of the whole city in the warm months.

## The practical bit

- **Getting to Toulouse:** a huge hub — TGV from Paris, budget airlines to Blagnac airport, trains all over.
- **Getting around:** the metro and tramwork the city; for the beach + festival + canal it's all walkable / tramable.
- **Timing:** aim midsummer; check the Rio Loco and 14 July dates (they move year to year).

[IMAGE]

## What I'd do differently

I'd plan the week to catch the 14-Juillet fireworks — the single most wounding memory of the whole trip — and let the rest assemble freely around late Tan-tan river evenings. It's not a list city; it's a *sunset city*. Follow the sun.
