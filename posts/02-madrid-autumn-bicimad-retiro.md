# Madrid in Autumn on a Bicycle: Retiro, Rooftops & the Bicimad Hack

[IMAGE]

Autumn is Madrid's best season. The light turns low and golden, the heat finally breaks, and the parks turn shades of ochre that no-one photographs enough. There's no better way to drift through it — stop for a hot chocolate, circle a palace, cut through an orchard — than on a rented city bike.

The system is called **Bicimad**, and it will be your whole strategy. Here's what I wish someone had told me before I started.

## The Bicimad hack: register in the morning

[IMAGE]

Bicimad is Madrid's public bike-share. Cheap, thousands of docks, electric assist on the hills. The catch is the registration, and the catch has a catch.

**Tip that saves you an hour of your life:** register in the morning. Late at night — and especially around midnight, when the daytime tourist rush ends and the system syncs — the app and web portal get genuinely slow, error-laden, and frustrating. One click stalls, the session times out, you're locked in a loop. Do it at 9am with a coffee and it's minutes, not a wrestling match.

**Second tip: it works with international cards.** A lot of European bike-share systems refuse non-domestic bank cards or demand SMS verification to a local number. Bicimad handles international cards fine, and the app accepts the standard card fields without a Spanish phone number. Worth knowing before you assume it'll be a wall.

The steps: download the app → register with ID and card → buy a short pass (they do casual visitor passes — you don't need a monthly plan) → unlock, ride, dock. Simple, once the system is awake.

**A note on the actual roads:** the old city is busy and the bike lanes are not consistent. You'll share some streets with buses and taxis. Pace yourself, expect the occasional uphill, and dock everywhere.

## Retiro — the jewel you came for

[IMAGE]

**Parque del Retiro** is the headline, and it deserves it. On a weekday in autumn the crowds you'd have in summer are gone, and the big trees are turning.

Some favourites:

- The **Palacio de Cristal** — the glass palace, and on weekday mornings you can have it nearly to yourself. The light through the glass in October is pure gold.
- **El Estanque** — the great artificial lake, the monument to Alfonso XII, and the rowboats. Renting a boat here is a cliché you should absolutely do once.
- The **Rosaleda** (rose garden) and the quieter orchard-wood edges — where the real Madrid walks its dogs and runs.
- The **Crystal Palace's** sibling, the **Palacio de Velázquez**, free exhibitions, often superb.

You can't take Bicimad inside the park — but you dock just at the edge (there's a good station at the gate) and walk in. That's the whole system working for you.

## Where the bike genuinely wins

[IMAGE]

Some Madrid itineraries are better on foot; Retiro-autumn is not one of them. A bike turns "a morning and an afternoon" into a full loop — you roll around parks, glide along the **Paseo del Prado**, aim for the **Gran Vía** rooftops, and the city becomes affectionate rather than tiring. Dock and walk the final stretch to the Museo del Prado, then pick up another bike and drift to a **tostón & coffee** terrace.

The autumn light hits the **Serrano** streets, the brick and stone of the old centre, the bridge over the Manzanares — all of it photogenic in that low season gold.

## The practical bit

- **Registrar in the morning** — non-negotiable, unless you enjoy shouting at your phone at midnight.
- **International cards are fine;** bring your normal card.
- **Passes:** start with the casual single-ride / short-term pass; buy more if you fall in love (you will).
- **Dock at the gate** of Retiro, not inside.
- **When to go:** mid-morning, after coffee, before the golden hour traffic. You want sunlight, not peak tourism.

[IMAGE]

**What I'd do differently:** start at Retiro, not fight it at the end. It's the reason you came. And leave the evening for the rooftops on foot, with a flamenco-adjacent drink, not while pedaling.
