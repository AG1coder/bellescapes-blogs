# The Pyrenees by Train from Toulouse: Cascades, Lakes, and the Great Luchon

[IMAGE]

There's a version of the Pyrenees you have to earn by driving — and a version you can reach by **train from Toulouse**, which is the secret most people skip. A day trip into the high mountain air, waterfalls, thermal towns, and cold green lakes, all on a line that runs right up the mountain valleys.

I rode from Toulouse into the mountains: **Aulus-les-Bains, the cascades, Luchon, and the great lakes.** This is that day.

## Getting there: the mountain train from Toulouse

[IMAGE]

The journey *is* part of the experience. The line climbs from the low country into the valley, past villages that cling to the slopes. The key stops:

- The **line toward the stations of the Ariège and the Haute-Garonne**, with regional trains and TER lines.
- It's scenic from the first crossing of the hills; by the time you reach the mountain stations you're already inside the landscape.

Check the current (2026) TER schedules — some mountain reach runs morning-peak, and you want the early one.

## Aulus-les-Bains — the thermal village in the woods

[IMAGE]

Aulus is a quiet thermal village at the foot of high valley, the perfect first stop: old baths, streams, forest immediately around. Uphill from it is one of the great small hikes of the Ariège.

## The cascades: Cascade de Lys and Cascade d'Os

[IMAGE]

Two waterfalls carry the valley breath. **Cascade de Lys**, an easy walk to a tall, elegant drop. And further, **Cascade d'O** — smaller, forested, cool. Both are increasingly peaceful after the short feeder climbs; the air, the spray, the sound. The path is good; take water and a wind layer even in summer (mountain air above the town).

## Les Lacs — Lac de Gaube and the high basins

[IMAGE]

The lakes are the reward. **Lac de Gaube** is a glacial-blue basin ringed by high summits — you can reach it by a solid half-day climb (or the lift part of the gondola if you're not fit; check the operator and season). Above, hidden tarns across the high valley.

**Luchon (Bagnères-de-Luchon)** — the big mountain resort town, the charming thermal base, great for an afternoon resting, and the eastern anchor for activities and the lift/walks.

## Activities from Luchon and around

- **Bathing & thermal:** Luchon and Ax-les-Thermes and the valley spas.
- **Hiking** from the big basins (the high and the easy walks both).
- **The lift/gondola** up from Luchon toward the high ridges (the view into the valley is everything).
- **Ax-les-Thermes & the neighbouring valleys** — more thermal plus the spectacular easy reach of the lakes.

## The practical bit

- **Train from Toulouse** — the TER; you need the morning schedule and a day-pass/return.
- **Base:** Toulouse day-trip is real and very possible; two days lets you also do the deep lake walks.
- **Gear:** trail shoes & a light waterproof — the mountain changes weather in 20 minutes.
- **Season:** July-September for dry trails; spring for waterfalls at their fullest (but mud).

## What I'd do differently

I'd take two days and hand the second one to **Luchon** only: morning café in town, then the lift + a high walk, then the hot bath. The atlas-drained version (cascades + lakes in one frantic afternoon) is doable but you miss the mountain's point, which is to slow down to its pace.
