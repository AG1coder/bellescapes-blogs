# Feria Season: Three Basque Towns, Three Very Different Fiestas

[IMAGE]

There is a version of feria that you will only understand by standing in it. Not on a screen, not in a guidebook — in the middle of a crowd that is two parts music, one part anticipation, and all of it beaming.

I wanted to see how the same celebration changes when the town changes. So I went to three of them: a city, a big town, and a small village — **Pampelune (Pamplona)**, **Dax**, and **Saint-Vincent-de-Tyrosse**. Same culture, three completely different hearts.

## Pampelune — the big-city feria, on full volume

[IMAGE]

Let's be honest: Pamplona is enormous. The festival here is a week-long institution, famous for the "encierro" (the running of the bulls), and the energy is bigger than almost anything you'll see in the south of France. White and red everywhere, marching bands, hundreds of thousands of people.

**The good:** it's overwhelming in the best possible way. There are day events for children, free concerts drifting from the squares, and the old walled streets around the cathedral transform completely.

**The trick:** the crowds are real and they arrive from Friday. Book accommodation months ahead, or stay in a neighbouring town and take the train in each morning — it's a 20-minute ride and you'll sleep better.

I arrived mid-morning. Streets already packed. I grabbed a café con leche at a pavement table and just watched three hundred people cross the square in white and red, and thought: this is the version they show on TV, and it's true.

**Three towns visited here? No — this is a full day.** My day ran: morning walls nothing food (traditional pimientos + grilled in a sidrería), afternoon standing in the plaza where the running starts, evening joining the free band concert. Twelve hours, 25,000 steps.

## Dax — the big town with the thermal soul

[IMAGE]

An hour and a half north of Pampelune, across the border, Dax. Big enough to have a proper feria, small enough to run into the same family six times in one afternoon.

Dax is a spa town — the famous thermal springs, the *fontaine chaude* in the middle of everything. So its feria treats better than some cities treat a whole month.

It's less overwhelming, more walkable, and — for a foreigner — much easier to dive into. People here welcome questions. They will tell you which band is about to pass, which bodega (temporary tent-bar) has the best tapas, and which hour to avoid the heat.

**The best thing I ate all trip is here:** the *brochettes* and the cold-cured *jambon* from the market tents after dark, with the crowd humming along to a brass band on a side street.

## Saint-Vincent-de-Tyrosse — the village feria

[IMAGE]

This is the one I think about most.

Saint-Vincent-de-Tyrosse is small. Really village. Yet in late summer it holds one of the liveliest little ferias in the region, and everybody from miles around shows up — but "everybody" here means a few thousand, not a few hundred thousand.

There are *courses & lǒ*, music, a little pedestrian street, kids chasing each other with water pistols, grandparents drinking rosé at the same café they've always drunk it at.

It's not about spectacle. It's about belonging. If you come to a village feria, you're family by the second song. **This is where you feel the language, the rhythm, the real thing** — the feria as community habit, not as headline.

[IMAGE]

## The practical bit

- **Best time:** late August — early September, when all three align.
- **Getting around:** Pampelune is easiest by air (Pamplona-Pirineos) or by train from the south of France; Dax and Saint-Vincent are both on the train line from the coast and to the interior. Rent nothing, learn the bus/train timing the night before.
- **Book ahead** for Pampelune accommodation — the two smaller towns have less pressure, but their good guesthouses fill before the weekend.
- **To fit in most:** get there before dark, walk the town gates, find the smallest square, sit down, order something manageable, and don't leave for two hours.
