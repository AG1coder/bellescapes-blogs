# The South of France Coast: Three Towns, Three Different Seashores

[IMAGE]

The Mediterranean south of France hides its character in the details. Not one beach town, but hundreds — and the ones that matter are the ones with a soul you can touch. I spent days on a triangle: **Collioure**, hugging the foot of the Pyrenees; **Gruissan**, dreaming on its lagoon; and **Canet-en-Roussillon**, flat, wide, and golden.

Three towns, three versions of the "Côte" same sea.

## Collioure — the painter's port

[IMAGE]

Collioure is the postcard. And unlike many postcards, it deserves the photograph. The seafront with its two bell towers — the church on the water, the old castle — is one of the most beautiful in the south. Fauvist painters — Derain, Matisse — fell for it here, and you'll see why standing on the old quay at dawn.

[Sant Vicenç chapel, the towers, the castle hill.]

It's small, but it's deep with texture: the castle, the harbor of fishing boats, cobbled ramps, and the surprising artistic history. It's the kind of place you share happily, and then retreat to the quiet corners to find the real boats.

- **The castle** (Royal Castle of Collioure) — a genuine stone medieval wall over the sea.
- **The Notre-Dame-des-Anges** church with its pink-tinged bell tower on the waterfront.
- **The views south** toward the Pyrenees and down the coast; and toward Spain beyond.

**The best moment:** before the day-trippers flood in. Buy bread, sit at the harbor, watch the boats; the light turns Mikiko-gold and the town is entirely hers.

## Gruissan — the round lagoon town

[IMAGE]

If Collioure is a painter's palette, Gruissan is a fisherman's camp that went cinematic. Built around a round lagoon, with a famous dominant tower and the distinctive *chalet* development along the Playa — it has a peculiar, self-contained charm.

You don't walk Gruissan like a city. You wander it as a landscape — the port, the salt and vines, the tower, the odd wooden cabins on the lagoon edge.

- **The lighthouse-like tower** (the old watchtower) — a climb with a view across the lagoon to the sea.
- **The salt.** The lagoon and the salt space are what the town's soul floats on.
- Slow, sandy flats with a raw, unglitzed strand.

Gruissan is where you relax, not where you rush. Come alone, outdoor table, rosé, long gaze at the flats.

## Canet-en-Roussillon — the wide, straightforward beach town

The opposite end of the mood. Canet is a proper beach town — wide golden sand, gravel, twilight promenade, a resort feel that takes its beach seriously.

- **The long strand** — minutes of flat, good sand, family water, swimmable.
- **The promenade** — ice cream, people-watching.
- Easy day with café and the sea.

It's the mainstream of the three, but the mainstream here is *good*. If you want an easy, generous beach day without any mystical stairs, Canet delivers.

[IMAGE]

## The practical bit

- **Getting between them:** Collioure is ~30 min south of Perpignan by train; Gruissan and Canet are separate beach towns north-east of Perpignan — so base yourself in Perpignan and day-trip them, or drive if you want the sand between.
- **Base:** Perpignan is the hub; good enough day trips, and in summer there's the coastal train & a light bus service to the beaches.
- **Connections:** trains to Perpignan from Montpellier and Fig due south; and Perpignan to Collioure is the ideal rail escape.
- **When:** the towns glow in late afternoon; the mornings are easier in heat and crowds.

## What I'd do differently

I'd spend the night in Collioure, and wake up to its dawn. Group the other two into a calmer, deliberately slow day — they reward you for not rushing, what they give a checklist is a mood.
