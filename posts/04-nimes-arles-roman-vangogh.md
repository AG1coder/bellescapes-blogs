# Nîmes & Arles: Roman Stone and Van Gogh's Light

[IMAGE]

Two towns, forty minutes apart, joined by more than a road. Both Roman. Both obsessed with light and art. Together they make one of the most satisfying two-day trips in the south of France — and neither is a compromise for the other.

## Nîmes — the best-preserved Roman city in France

[IMAGE]

Nîmes is a Roman city in a way few places in Europe still are. Not just ruins — a working town built around a nearly complete Roman theatre.

**The Arena (Les Arènes).** One of the best-preserved Roman amphitheatres anywhere, holding around 24,000 in its day. It's enormous, precise, and you can walk its whole structure and galleries. The acoustics are still so good they host concerts there in summer. This is the showpiece.

**The Maison Carrée.** The strikingly intact Roman temple, fine, columned, elegant — and still standing on its original plaza, now with a modern glass cinema building beside it (a fun contrast of the ancient and contemporary).

**The Museums.** Nîmes over-delivers for a city its size:
- **Musée de la Romanité** — the modern, sleek museum across from the Arena, full of Roman artefacts and smartly designed. The building itself is part of the draw.
- **Musée des Beaux-Arts** for painting.
- The **Jardins de la Fontaine**, the gardens and the old historic hill — you can climb the **Tour Magne** for a panorama over the rooftops.

[LIVE: The Jardin de la Fontaine, the tower, the old green park.]

**The feeling.** Nîmes is grand and self-assured. It doesn't need your attention — it has been here two thousand years and will be here after you. Best mid-afternoon, when the light hits the golden stone and the café terraces take over the square across from the Arena.

## Arles — Van Gogh and the museums

[IMAGE]

Across, Arles is smaller, dustier, more lifted-by-art. This is where Van Gogh painted in a frenzy in 1888-89 — the starry nights, the sunflowers, the café at night — before he went wrong at the end.

**The Van Gogh connection** is everywhere but done tastefully. You can follow the spots where his subjects stood — the **Place du Forum** with the Café de la Nuit (recreated), the river, the night sky.

**The Museums:**
- The **Fondation Van Gogh**, in a 15th-century mansion — permanent pieces and changing shows framed around his Arles time.
- The **Musée de l'Arles et la Provence Antiques** — Romanesque — also strong for classical.
- And the wildcard the user named: **the Gehry-designed museum.** This is the **LUMA Arles** — Frank Gehry's astonishing, corkscrewing, titanium tower of dry-stone and glass in the Parc des Ateliers. Free, modern, sometimes controversial, always worth seeing. It anchors the contemporary-art scene and utterly breaks with the Roman sense two miles away. It's a one-of-a-kind building.

## The practical bit

- **Getting between them:** ~20-30 min by train — Arles is on the main line and Nîmes is a hub. Very easy; one base works for day-tripping the other, though staying one night in each is lovelier.
- **Base:** both are well served from Avignon and Marseille (TGV).
- **One day vs two:** two is right. Nîmes = stone & Roman. Arles = light & Van Gogh & museums. They don't compete; they complete.

## What I'd do differently

I'd start the morning in Arles when the town is quiet, warm east-west light on the forum, then take the train to Nîmes for a golden hour at the Arena and the turquoise of the Jardins de la Fontaine. That dual-day rhythm is as good as it gets.
