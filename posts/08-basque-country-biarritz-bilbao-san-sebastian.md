# Basque Country: Biarritz, Bilbao, San Sebastián & the Flysch of Zumaia

[IMAGE]

Few regions in Europe pack so much personality into so little geography as the Basque Country. Four stops, four completely different moods — a French surf town, an industrial city reborn through architecture, a pintxo capital of Europe, and a raw geological marvel on the coast. I could write a book; here's the part worth your one trip.

## Biarritz — the stylish French coast

[IMAGE]

Biarritz is where the French Riviera energy meets the Atlantic surf. The old lighthouses and the cliff promenade, the **Grande Plage**, the **Rocher de la Vierge** — a tiny rocky chapel-marker on the headland you walk out to. Beautiful, photogenic, with a strong surf culture on my weekends.

**Best for:** the walk, the lighthouse, the light on the Bay of Biscay at sunset. And for breakfast pastry on a sea terrace before the crowd.

**Mood:** the most *polished* of the four. Elegant, well kept.

## Bilbao — the industrial giant reborn by the Guggenheim

[IMAGE]

Bilbao in one word is **revolution**. The Guggenheim museum, Frank Gehry again (yes — the same architect of the Arles tower), transformed this former industrial city into an architectural pilgrimage in the 1990s. Outside-in the river the **Guggenheim Bilbao** is a sculptural monument of titanium curves. Inside, world-class.

But Bilbao keeps its grit. The **Casco Viejo** (old town) — the narrow lanes, the pintxos bars, the **Mercado**. The riverwalk. The funicular up Artxanda. It's a city of character, not just a museum.

**Best for:** the museum, the old town, the food-scene night.

## San Sebastián (Donostia) — arguably the food & beach capital

[IMAGE]

A beautiful bay beach town, home of the **Pintxos** — the little toasts — and to the highest concentration of Michelin stars per resident on Earth. The **Playa de la Concha**, the bay shaped like a shell, surrounded by hills, is one of the most beautiful beach bays in Europe.

**Food is the attraction:** the old-town pintxos crawl is a rite. Blur; the formal meals are some of the planet's best. Budget for food like it's the main event — because it is.

**Best for:** the beach, the bay, the food, the walk up Monte Igueldo for the sunset over the whole crescent.

## The Flysch of Zumaia — a geological wonder

[IMAGE]

And then, quietly, the star *outside* the big three: the **Flysch of Zumaia** on the coast south of San Sebastián — cliffs of near-vertical layered rock, each layer a chapter of hundreds of millions of years of Earth history. The **beach at Itzurun**, the tilted strata, the dramatic tilted layers of stratification — wildly photogenic and geologically profound.

**The wave** (rock platform) at low tide exposes the fossil floorbands. Best at sunrise or sunset for the rock texture.

**Access:** a short bus/road from Zumaia station; it's a distinct, raw change of pace from the towns.

## The practical bit

- **Movement:** the Basque is compact enough that a base + a couple of legs works best. The **Euskotren (coastal train)** links the three coastal spots well; Bilbao is ~1h from the coast.
- **Ordering:** Biarritz → San Sebastián → Zumaia → Bilbao is a sane anti-clockwise loop, or start at Bilbao and end at Biarritz for a beach-rising finale.
- **Pace:** three days minimum for flavour, four to five for comfort.

## What I'd do differently

Don't try all four in stray afternoons. Pick two souls: San Sebastián for the food & bay **and** the Flysch for the raw geology, and keep Bilbao — which deserves a full day it won't get squeezed — to its own trip with Biarritz. Overloading the Ganbegey jumps too fast between modern shimmer and raw coast; each deserves its own me.
